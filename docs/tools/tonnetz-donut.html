Euler's Tonnetz, on a donut

The Tonnetz ("tone network") lays the 12 notes on a triangular grid. Here one direction steps by perfect fifths (around the ring), one by minor thirds (around the tube) and the diagonals by major thirds.

Every small triangle is a triad: warm = major, cool = minor. Neighbouring triangles share two notes, and crossing an edge is one of the neo‑Riemannian moves: P (parallel, C ↔ Cm), L (leading‑tone, C ↔ Em), R (relative, C ↔ Am).

Treat C♯ and D♭ as the same note and the flat grid wraps round in both directions, so it closes up into a torus: 12 notes, 24 triads. This donut shows the grid four times over, so each chord appears four times, and they all light up together.

Orbit: drag to tumble the donut, pinch or scroll to zoom, two‑finger twist to roll it. Skin: drag sideways to slide the skin around the ring, up and down to roll it through the hole. Flip (or double‑tap) rolls the skin half a turn through the centre.

Walk: become a little character on the surface. Left thumb (or WASD) walks, right thumb (or Q/E) turns the camera. Each tile you step on sounds its chord, voiced to move as smoothly as possible from the last one.
