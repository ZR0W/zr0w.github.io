Overview

Tilebound is an endless, turn-based dungeon crawl on a grid 7 squares wide. You lead a squad of four heroes upward through procedurally generated rows. Nothing moves until you act; then every piece on screen takes one step by simple rules, like a cellular automaton. The run ends when every hero has fallen.

  • The view shows 11 rows. Your heroes always live in the bottom 4 rows, the gold deployment zone.
  • Depth counts how many rows you have advanced. The dungeon's world rows are numbered from the start, so a row numbered N first appears at the top of the screen when depth ≈ N − 10. Unlocks in this wiki are given as rows.
  • Pass ?seed=123 in the game's URL for a reproducible dungeon.

Turn order

One action per turn. Everything after step 1 resolves automatically.

  1. Choose — Forward, Left, Right, Hold, or reposition/swap one hero inside the zone. Repositioning spends the turn.
  2. Move — heroes that can move do (see Movement rules). Stepping onto a chest, potion, shrine or raised spikes resolves now.
  3. Heroes strike — one hero at a time: the attacker glows in its colour, its skill name pops up and its whole pattern flashes; every enemy in the pattern is hit (the Ranger and Turret hit only the first). Druid and Engineer build here.
  4. Enemies act — every enemy on screen follows its rule, closest to the squad first, against the live board. Each attack plays on its own. Badges show what each enemy will do next: ↓ ← → ↑ move, ! attack, · idle.
  5. World acts — spike traps toggle, boulders roll, vine walls age.

Movement rules

The squad moves together, but each hero only moves if its target square is free (or is being vacated by a teammate who moves).

Turrets move with the squad but never block a move — a stuck turret is simply left behind. Refused moves never cost a turn, and the status line says why.

Heroes

Pick 4 on the party screen; pick order sets the starting spots (first two in front). Pattern grids: gold = the hero, blue = squares it strikes, forward is up.

Bestiary

Enemies attack by stepping into a hero (bump), except the archer, which shoots. All of them avoid walls, items (except spikes), heroes and each other.

Spawning & scaling

Items & interactables

At most one random item per generated row, rolled on a free square. Structures can place extra ones.

Terrain

Walls, rocks and pillars all block heroes and enemies the same way; only the look differs.

Structures

Rows are generated one structure at a time, each followed by an empty row. A structure is kept only if every row has an opening at least 2 squares wide (room for the 2×2 golem) and the open squares connect from the row below to its top; otherwise it is re-rolled (an empty row after 6 failures). The first 6 rows are always empty. Maps below are examples; real ones vary.

Scoring

Score = depth × 10 + kill points + gold. Kill points are listed in the Bestiary; chests give 25 gold. Your best score is kept in this browser.

Tips

  • Watch the intent badges before you commit: a red ! on your hero means it will be hit this turn.
  • Funnel through wall gaps by sidestepping first — squished heroes catch up when the others stop.
  • A hero stuck on the bottom row blocks Forward; reposition it (one turn) or sidestep.
  • The Guardian beside a fragile hero (Mage, Rogue, Bombardier) soaks their hits.
  • Golems can't fit through 1-wide gaps; vines and pillars make good choke points.
